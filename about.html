What is Shooting Stars?

Shooting Stars is an online platform where independent filmmakers can manage their projects, track their progress and find the people they need, all in one place. It is built to feel like a simple version of what an agency does for a filmmaker, without the judgement or the hassle.

The name comes from a show about the people who work behind the screen to make a film or a show possible. The idea is that everyone has a right to be a shooting star.

The problem it solves

Independent filmmakers often work alone or in very small teams, and they struggle with five things:

  • Managing the work: scripts, budgets, schedules, cast and edits are spread across many places.
  • Being seen: without a studio, it is hard for audiences to find their films.
  • Finding people: actors, editors, composers and crew are hard to find on a small budget.
  • Money: planning a budget and finding funding is the biggest barrier.
  • Getting released: festivals have deadlines, fees and rules that are easy to lose track of.

Features

  • Add and save projects with a title, genre, creator, year, synopsis and an uploaded image.
  • Progress tracking: every step of making a film, from the idea to release, with a percentage ring for each project and rings for completed projects and average progress.
  • Project page: open any project to update how much of the script is written, whether the cast is decided, filming, editing and more.
  • Budget tracker: set a total, add expenses and see what is left.
  • Festival deadlines: keep track of submissions, fees, status and days left.
  • Feedback: keep comments from viewers and collaborators on each project.
  • Browse movies and shows: search any title to see its image, creators, cast and synopsis.
  • Upcoming festivals: a list of upcoming film festivals with countdowns, and a button to add a festival to one of your projects.
  • Find Crew: post a call for cast and crew, or browse calls from other filmmakers. With the free Firebase option switched on, calls are shared between all visitors.
  • Filmmaker Guide: short notes on funding, contracts, music licences, copyright, festivals and distribution.

How it is built

  • HTML, CSS and JavaScript only, with no frameworks.
  • localStorage saves projects, progress and uploaded images in the browser, so no backend is needed.
  • data.json is a pre-filled file of sample films shown in Browse.
  • festivals.json is the list of upcoming festivals. It is updated by editing the file, and finished festivals hide themselves.
  • TVMaze and OMDb APIs provide the show and movie search results.
  • Firebase Firestore (optional) stores the shared crew calls so every visitor sees the same board.
  • GitHub Pages hosts the finished website.

How Find Crew works

  • A filmmaker fills in the role they need, the project, the pay and a contact detail, then posts the call.
  • Other filmmakers browse the board and filter it by role, then contact the person directly.
  • On its own, a static website cannot share data between visitors. In demo mode a call is saved only in the browser that posted it.
  • With the free Firebase option switched on, calls are stored online, so every visitor sees the same board. Posted calls cannot be edited or deleted by visitors.

Limitations and future improvements

Projects, progress, budgets and feedback are saved only in the browser they are used in, because the site has no user accounts. A future version could add logins and a full database, so that projects and feedback could be shared too, and could link to crowdfunding and festival websites.
