Filmmaker Guide

Plain-language basics. This is general information, not legal advice.

Funding your film
  • Crowdfunding: pitch your film to an audience and offer rewards.
  • Grants and film funds from arts councils, film bodies and festivals.
  • Local sponsors or businesses who want to support a project.
  • Co-producers and investors, who usually expect a share of any profit.

Track your spending in the Budget section of each project page.

Contracts and agreements
  • Put every agreement in writing: crew, cast, writers, composers and editors.
  • Get a signed release from every person who appears on camera.
  • Get written permission for every location you film in.
  • State clearly who owns the work, who gets credit, and how and when people are paid.
Music licences
  • Using a commercial song needs permission for the recording and for the composition.
  • Safer options: music you commission, royalty-free libraries, or Creative Commons tracks whose terms you follow.
  • Keep a copy of every licence in case a festival or platform asks for proof.
Copyright
  • You usually hold the copyright in your own work, but work made by others needs a written agreement.
  • Do not use footage, images, fonts or music without checking their licence.
  • Credit everyone who contributed.
Festivals and distribution
  • Check each festival’s deadline, fee and rules, such as premiere requirements.
  • Track them in the Festivals section of each project page.
  • After festivals, consider streaming platforms, online video sites or your own screenings.
